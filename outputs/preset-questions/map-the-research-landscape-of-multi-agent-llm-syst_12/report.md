# Research Report

## Research Question

Map the research landscape of multi-agent LLM systems.

## Summary

This report explores the landscape of multi-agent systems utilizing large language models (LLMs), detailing the architectural patterns, their interrelations, and identifying open problems within the research space.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Multi-agent systems leveraging LLMs can be categorized into various architectural patterns.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Strong foundational studies conducted on multi-agent LLM architectures provide comprehensive categorizations.

**Evidence**

- Current architectures can be broadly classified into cooperative, competitive, and hybrid models, each exhibiting unique interaction protocols and decision-making processes.
- Cooperative models often utilize joint task planning, while competitive models may employ adversarial setups; hybrid models combine both approaches for diversified applications.

### Finding 2

**Claim**

The relationship between different architectural patterns reveals potential avenues for enhancement and innovation.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Preliminary findings on inter-architecture relationships are emerging, but more empirical evidence is needed.

**Evidence**

- Particularly, interfaces between cooperative and competitive models could lead to novel approaches in negotiation and conflict resolution in multi-agent settings.
- Common challenges such as coordination, communication overhead, and knowledge sharing can be efficiently tackled using a hybrid approach.

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- The lack of standardized metrics for evaluating the performance of multi-agent LLM systems leads to inconsistencies in research findings.
- Limited understanding of human-like reasoning and ethical considerations in negotiation strategies among multi-agent systems.

## Remaining Gaps

- Exploration of real-world deployment scenarios for multi-agent LLM systems.
- Integration of ethical considerations into system design and interaction protocols.
- Empirical assessment of multi-agent systems in various application domains such as healthcare and finance.

## Conclusion

The research landscape of multi-agent LLM systems is rich with potential, offering various architectural patterns that can be leveraged collaboratively or competitively. However, significant gaps remain in practical application, ethical frameworks, and standardized evaluation metrics. Future work should focus on addressing these gaps to foster advancement in the field of multi-agent systems powered by large language models.
