## Comprehensiveness

### R1

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.75
- Rationale: This is substantial treatment with unusually clear distinction between nameplate/process capture and facility-level performance, including an operating period and monitoring evidence. It falls short of full coverage because actual sustained availability and net emissions reductions are not comprehensively demonstrated across representative projects.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report distinguishes operational capacity from global emissions, reporting approximately 50–51 MtCO₂ per year and roughly 0.16% of energy and industrial-process emissions.
  - It gives Petra Nova evidence over a three-year demonstration period: approximately 3.54 million tonnes captured, a design target of at least 90%, and reported process-level capture efficiency of 92.4%.
  - It contrasts process-level estimates with facility-level estimates of approximately 65–70%, or 55–58% including cogeneration emissions, and notes that the project went offline in 2020.
  - It reports monitoring and accounting at Petra Nova, including 3.61 million short tons sequestered out of 3.64 million captured under the project accounting method.
  - The report explicitly notes that capture-rate, uptime, lifecycle, whole-facility, and long-term climate metrics are different measures.
- Missing:
  - It provides little representative measured evidence from projects other than Petra Nova, such as sustained operating availability, annual captured-versus-designed quantities, or actual net emissions reductions for multiple sectors.
  - It does not quantify lifecycle emissions, parasitic energy emissions, avoided emissions, or net atmospheric reductions for the cited projects.
  - The global operational-capacity figures are presented with unresolved discrepancies and are not systematically separated into capture, transport, permanent storage, and utilization.
