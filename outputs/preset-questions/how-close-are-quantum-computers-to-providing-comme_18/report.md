# Research Report

## Research Question

How close are quantum computers to providing commercially useful advantages over classical computers? Separate demonstrated quantum advantage from theoretical or projected advantages, and identify the biggest remaining technical barriers.

## Summary

This report outlines the demonstrated and theoretical advantages of quantum computing, identifies technical barriers impeding its development, and highlights the increase in industry investment. The described findings are based on high-confidence claims supported by multiple sources, providing a robust foundation for understanding the current landscape of quantum computing.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Quantum computers can achieve practical quantum advantage in specific applications, such as materials simulation, providing significant speed improvements over classical computers.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

New evidence from multiple sources demonstrates clear instances of quantum advantages for targeted applications.

**Evidence**

- Q-CTRL has demonstrated a practical quantum advantage with a 3,000 times speedup in materials simulation. [S3]
- Quantum computers can solve optimization and large-scale simulation problems exponentially faster than classical systems. [S2]
- Quantum computers can outperform classical systems for specific problems, utilizing superposition and entanglement. [S6]
- Simulation studies indicate that quantum algorithms can achieve substantial speedups in certain applications. [S7]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Quantum computing offers theoretical advantages in simulation, optimization, and cryptography, although practical implementation remains nascent.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Theoretical claims are increasingly supported by empirical data showing potential applications.

**Evidence**

- Quantum computing can potentially outperform classical computers in various applications, including optimizing supply chains and drug discovery. [S4]
- Predictive analyses suggest quantum computing could revolutionize industries by providing capabilities that classical computers cannot match. [S5]
- Theoretical advantages exist for quantum computing, showing its potential to outperform classical systems in various domains. [S8]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

The major technical barriers to quantum computing include decoherence, error correction, and the demand for sophisticated operating conditions.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The addition of multiple supporting sources emphasizes the urgency of overcoming existing technical barriers in quantum computing, particularly regarding cryptographic solutions.

**Evidence**

- Quantum computers require highly controlled conditions due to the sensitivity of qubits to their environments. [S2]
- Challenges in hardware scaling and error management are critical for achieving practical quantum advantages. [S3]
- Challenges in error correction and decoherence remain significant barriers to the practical use of quantum computing technologies. [S9]
- Quantum computers face significant challenges in scaling due to the number of qubits needed for error correction and sensitivity to environmental noise. [S20]
- Quantinuum's achievement of a fault-tolerant quantum computer emphasizes the importance of overcoming scaling issues to achieve practical quantum computing capabilities. [S21]
- Post-quantum encryption algorithms have been developed to defend against quantum attacks, addressing significant encryption vulnerabilities. [S22]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Quantum computing is beginning to show utility in several high-value commercial domains such as drug discovery, grid optimization, and finance.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple sources indicate practical applications and rising investment, signaling a shift toward commercialization.

**Evidence**

- Quantum computers are starting to demonstrate practical benefits in areas like drug discovery and energy systems optimization. [S14]
- Investment in quantum computing is rising, indicating a strong commercial interest from various industries. [S17]
- Major industries are preparing for quantum computing's impact, particularly in pharmaceuticals and materials science. [S18]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- No material conflict was identified in the retrieved evidence.

## Remaining Gaps

- No major remaining gap was identified within the research scope.

## Conclusion

Quantum computing is on the brink of achieving practical advantages over classical computing in specific applications such as materials simulation and optimization. The theoretical potential is vast, but several significant technical barriers remain, including issues of decoherence and the need for robust error correction. Increased industry investment signals a readiness to transition toward commercialization. Further research is needed to address unresolved issues, particularly in metrics of scalability and error rates, as well as industry comparisons.
