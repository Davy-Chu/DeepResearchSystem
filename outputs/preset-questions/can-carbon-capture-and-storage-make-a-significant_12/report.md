# Research Report

## Research Question

Can carbon capture and storage make a significant contribution to reducing global CO₂ emissions? Evaluate its demonstrated effectiveness, costs, scalability, energy requirements, and major arguments for and against large-scale deployment.

## Summary

The effectiveness of carbon capture and storage (CCS) in reducing global CO₂ emissions has been called into question. Despite substantial investments, CCS currently captures less than 0.1% of global emissions, with costs per ton varying widely. Its viability as a large-scale climate solution is challenged by high energy demands and limited geological storage options. However, CCS is deemed necessary for certain hard-to-abate sectors like cement and steel, raising a complex debate about its role versus reliance on renewable alternatives.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

CCS has captured less than 0.1% of annual global CO₂ emissions, raising serious concerns about efficacy as a climate solution.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Consistent evidence from multiple reliable sources.

**Evidence**

- CCS technology captures only a small fraction of global CO₂ emissions, indicating its limited effectiveness as a climate solution. [S1] [S8]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Implementation costs for CCS technology are high, estimated between $50 and $600 per ton of CO₂ captured.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Strong corroboration from various credible sources.

**Evidence**

- Cost estimates for CCS vary widely, with figures typically ranging from $50 to $600 per ton, influenced by technology choices. [S1] [S4]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

CCS is critical for achieving net-zero emissions, especially in sectors like cement and steel where emissions are hard to eliminate.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple authoritative sources affirm the necessity of CCS.

**Evidence**

- Experts widely agree that CCS is essential to deep decarbonization in industries with difficult-to-eliminate emissions. [S5] [S4]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

CCS implementation faces substantial challenges including high energy demands and limited availability of suitable geological storage sites.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Challenges are recognized, but detailed feasibility varies among sources.

**Evidence**

- Substantial energy requirements (2.5–8 GJ per ton of CO₂) and the limited availability of geological formations pose challenges to CCS implementation. [S1] [S10]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

There is an ongoing debate regarding CCS: some argue it prolongs fossil fuel reliance; others assert it is necessary for emissions reduction.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Competing views on CCS effectiveness create uncertainty.

**Evidence**

- Advocacy exists for both sides: while some sources highlight CCS as essential for emissions reduction, others suggest it may detract from transitioning to renewable energy. [S1] [S9]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- While some sources advocate for CCS as a strategic solution, evidence suggests its failures to meet expectations and advocates for focusing on alternatives. [S2] [S1]
- Economic viability of CCS is contested; critics argue it detracts from renewable energy investment while proponents emphasize its role in emissions reduction. [S4] [S9]

## Remaining Gaps

- What are the long-term effects of CO₂ storage on geological formations and surrounding environments?
- How can CCS be effectively integrated with renewable energy sources to enhance its viability?
- What alternative technologies could provide competitive carbon reduction capabilities without the risks associated with CCS?

## Conclusion

CCS presents significant potential for mitigating hard-to-abate emissions but is accompanied by notable challenges in deployment, effectiveness, cost, and safety. Understanding and addressing these challenges is essential for its role in the future energy landscape.
