# Research Report

## Research Question

Can carbon capture and storage make a significant contribution to reducing global CO₂ emissions? Evaluate its demonstrated effectiveness, costs, scalability, energy requirements, and major arguments for and against large-scale deployment.

## Summary

This report evaluates the various claims regarding carbon capture and storage (CCS), including its effectiveness, costs, scalability, and the debates surrounding its large-scale deployment. The findings present a comprehensive view of both supporting and contradicting evidence, highlighting the ongoing debates in the CCS discourse.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) captures and stores less than 0.1% of global CO₂ emissions, raising questions about its efficacy as a climate solution.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The additional evidence further confirms existing concerns about CCS efficacy and associated risks, reinforcing the current high confidence status.

**Evidence**

- Despite significant investment, CCS has captured a minimal percentage of global emissions, suggesting questions about its overall effectiveness. [S1]
- The review provides a comprehensive analysis of CCS technologies, focusing on the integrity and safety of storage sites, indicating a serious concern for its overall effectiveness in addressing global emissions. [S10]
- The blog post discusses the effectiveness of CCS, noting that it captures minimal emissions, but emphasizes the need for comprehensive safety assessments, thus raising questions about its efficacy. [S14]
- The review discusses the main barriers hindering widespread CCS adoption, emphasizing the uncertainties about the long-term behavior of stored CO2 and technical challenges. [S16]
- The article highlights environmental hazards associated with CCS technologies, raising further questions about their effectiveness and safety. [S17]
- Pipelines used in CCS have led to significant health and environmental incidents, raising serious concerns about their overall safety and efficacy. [S19]
- Amine-based CCS can lead to emissions of harmful pollutants, such as ammonia, which impact human health and environment, reinforcing concerns regarding CCS technologies. [S22]
- The IEEFA highlights that no current CCS project has consistently achieved target capture rates, emphasizing ongoing doubts about the technology's efficacy. [S25]
- The investigation reveals strategies to boost carbon capture demand but notes a persistent deficit in meeting climate targets, highlighting concerns about CCS effectiveness. [S26]
- CCS has potential risks such as leakage and environmental impacts, reinforcing concerns about its overall efficacy. [S28]
- CCS technology prolongs dependence on fossil fuels and fails to adequately address environmental concerns. [S29]
- The potential hazards associated with CO2 in CCUS technology highlight serious risks that must be managed. [S30]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

CCS technology could contribute up to 14% of the global greenhouse gas emissions reductions needed by 2050.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While supporting evidence suggests doubts about achieving emissions reductions through CCS, it does not outright contradict the claim of its potential contribution, indicating mixed perspectives.

**Evidence**

- Carbon capture can achieve a significant portion of the needed emissions reductions to meet climate goals. [S5]
- The Department of Energy allocated over $12 billion to carbon management projects, indicating a significant potential contribution of CCS to emissions reductions in hard-to-abate sectors. [S23]
- Predictions indicate that CCS will capture up to 6% of global CO₂ emissions by 2050, showing its potential role in future emissions reductions. [S24]
- The analysis suggests a potential deficit in CCS capabilities to meet emissions reduction targets by 2030, addressing the claims of CCS's significant contribution to emissions reductions. [S26]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

The costs of implementing large-scale CCS can range from $50 to $600 per ton of CO₂ captured.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence aligns with existing economic assessments, reinforcing the financial viability concerns associated with CCS.

**Evidence**

- The review discusses the economic aspects of CCS, including the costs associated with implementation. [S1]
- Research emphasizes that ensuring reliable underground storage presents a significant challenge in well integrity, impacting the economic viability associated with CCS. [S12]
- The economic implications of CCS systems are discussed, highlighting costs and risks associated with health impacts and necessary regulatory measures. [S20]
- The costs and economic implications of CCS were discussed, indicating ongoing challenges and potential high costs in achieving carbon capture effectively. [S26]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

CCS is criticized for diverting investment from renewable energy and other effective climate solutions.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

New evidence supports existing assertions about CCS diverting funds from more effective climate solutions, strengthening the claim's validity.

**Evidence**

- Critics argue that reliance on CCS may delay advancements in more effective emissions reduction technologies. [S4]
- The review notes that acceptance of CCS technologies remains complex due to public concerns over their environmental impact, supporting the argument regarding investment diversion from renewable solutions. [S27]
- Critics argue that CCS technologies divert investment from renewable energy alternatives. [S29]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

The efficiency of CCS can capture up to 90% of CO₂ emissions from industrial processes and power plants, improving with technological advancements.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The supporting evidence aligns well with the efficiency claims, highlighting the potential improvements in technology to enhance capture rates.

**Evidence**

- CCS projects can currently capture up to 90% of CO₂ emissions and future improvements may enhance this further. [S7]
- CCS is considered significant for decarbonizing hard-to-abate sectors, crucial for improving CO₂ capture rates as technology advances. [S23]
- The review discusses CCS technological advancements that can significantly capture CO₂ emissions, potentially supporting the claim regarding CCS's efficiency. [S27]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

CCS is considered a promising technology for reducing greenhouse gas emissions, yet it faces commercial, technical, and regulatory challenges.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Additional evidence about commercial and regulatory challenges reaffirms the barriers faced by CCS technology.

**Evidence**

- CCS shows potential in emission reduction but encounters numerous challenges including costs and regulatory complexities. [S8]
- Despite being promising, CCS has significant cost and regulatory barriers that hinder its widespread adoption. [S9]
- The CCS Ladder report discusses how the economic viability challenges indicate that CCS faces significant integration issues within broader system transformations. These concerns align with existing claims about the hurdles faced by CCS technology. [S13]
- The paper discusses the challenges of CCS and highlights its necessity within an overall sustainable emission reduction strategy, reinforcing the consideration of CCS limitations and deployment scales. [S15]
- Technological, economic, and regulatory challenges for CCS are discussed, highlighting significant barriers that hinder broad adoption. [S16]
- The investigation emphasizes potential technological advancements and regulatory frameworks necessary for overcoming barriers to CCS adoption, aligning closely with the claim regarding challenges. [S27]
- CCS faces significant commercial and regulatory challenges which hinder its adoption despite its potential. [S29]

### Finding 7

**Claim**

Scaling up carbon capture and storage (CCS) could be crucial for achieving net-zero emissions, but significant policy, regulatory, and market actions are needed for its successful deployment.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence aligns well with the existing discussions regarding the crucial role of CCS in decarbonization and the pressing need for supportive policies.

**Evidence**

- The review provides a comprehensive evaluation of CCS technologies, emphasizing the need for scale-up and integration of CCS into various sectors to meet net-zero targets. [S31]
- Artificial Intelligence (AI) can enhance the effectiveness and scalability of CCS deployment, highlighting the importance of integrating advanced technologies in climate solutions. [S33]
- The article discusses the necessary conditions and requirements for CCS deployment to align with climate targets, outlining the need for rapid and extensive scaling. [S34]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- CCS's effectiveness in significantly capturing global CO₂ emissions is debated, with some sources supporting its progress and others highlighting major limitations. [S24] [S25]
- While CCS could contribute to emissions reductions, there are warnings about its limited scope and the risk of relying too heavily on the technology over renewables. [S26] [S4]

## Remaining Gaps

- No major remaining gap was identified within the research scope.

## Conclusion

The analysis indicates that while CCS has potential benefits for reducing CO₂ emissions, it currently captures only a small fraction of global emissions. High implementation costs and significant barriers related to technology, safety, and public acceptance pose challenges for large-scale deployment. There remain substantial discussions regarding the actual contribution of CCS to climate goals versus renewable energy solutions, making its role in future climate strategy complex and multifaceted. More comprehensive assessments and advancements in technology are necessary to enhance the role of CCS in global emissions reduction efforts.
