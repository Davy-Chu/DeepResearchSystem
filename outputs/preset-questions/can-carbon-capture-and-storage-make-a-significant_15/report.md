# Research Report

## Research Question

Can carbon capture and storage make a significant contribution to reducing global CO₂ emissions? Evaluate its demonstrated effectiveness, costs, scalability, energy requirements, and major arguments for and against large-scale deployment.

## Summary

This report evaluates carbon capture and storage (CCS) based on existing evidence regarding its effectiveness, costs, scalability, and arguments for and against its large-scale deployment. Key findings suggest that while CCS technology has potential, there are significant hurdles regarding its current effectiveness, economic viability, and impact on fossil fuel dependency.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) currently captures and stores less than 0.1% of annual global CO₂ emissions, questioning its effectiveness as a climate solution.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The increase in operational projects underscores a positive trend for CCS technology, affirming its potential advancements and effectiveness in reducing emissions.

**Evidence**

- Despite significant investment, CCS has not achieved its projected capture rates, capturing less than 0.1% of global CO2 emissions. [S1]
- Current CCUS technology captures approximately 64 million tonnes of CO₂ per year, but still addresses only a fraction of annual emissions. [S10]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology may enable continued fossil fuel use while aiming to reduce CO2 emissions.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Strong supporting evidence on CCS's role in reducing emissions while allowing fossil fuel use.

**Evidence**

- CCS allows fossil fuels to be used while significantly reducing emissions, especially in regions lacking renewables. [S2]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Challenges of implementing CCS include high costs, the necessity for suitable storage sites, and safety concerns.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The supporting evidence strongly emphasizes the high costs and complexities associated with CCS deployment.

**Evidence**

- CCS faces challenges like high implementation costs and limited availability of storage sites. [S2]
- The paper identifies significant barriers to CCS deployment, including high financial burdens. [S26]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

While CCS shows potential, it does not resolve the urgent need to phase out fossil fuels.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Concerns about reliance on fossil fuels while capturing CCS's limitations highlight its drawbacks.

**Evidence**

- Some argue that CCS does not significantly reduce fossil fuel dependency, undermining its overall efficacy in climate action. [S4]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

CCS currently captures around 0.1% of global CO₂ emissions, indicating a slow pace of adoption relative to climate targets.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Continued evidence supports the slow progress of CCS relative to what is necessary for climate targets.

**Evidence**

- Currently, CCUS captures approximately 0.1% of global emissions. [S7]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

CCS is projected to capture approximately 6% of global CO₂ emissions by 2050, a large increase from current levels.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Significant growth in CO₂ capture capacity enhances the projection.

**Evidence**

- Projections estimate that CCS technology could capture up to 6% of global CO2 emissions by 2050. [S9]

### Finding 7

**Claim**

The economic viability of CCS is improving due to expected reductions in costs driven by policy support.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The evidence highlights advancements in CCS and supportive policies which are expected to drive down costs.

**Evidence**

- Projected policy-driven growth in CCS capacity could lead to reductions in costs by about 14% by 2030. [S9]

### Finding 8

**Claim**

The carbon capture and storage (CCS) market is projected to experience significant growth, expanding from USD 8.30 billion in 2026 to USD 15.43 billion by 2036, with a CAGR of 6.4%.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The evidence provides a strong financial outlook for the CCS market.

**Evidence**

- The CCS market is forecast to grow significantly over the next decade. [S34]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- Some sources contend that CCS can capture over 90% of CO₂ from certain plants, while others assert that it is ineffective due to low global adoption. [S5] [S10]

## Remaining Gaps

- No major remaining gap was identified within the research scope.

## Conclusion

CCS presents both opportunities and challenges in addressing CO₂ emissions. While it holds potential for reducing emissions, its effectiveness and feasibility are challenged by economic, regulatory, and technological barriers. A balanced approach considering CCS alongside renewable energy solutions is essential for successful climate action.
