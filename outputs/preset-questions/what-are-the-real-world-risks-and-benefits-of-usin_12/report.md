# Research Report

## Research Question

What are the real-world risks and benefits of using synthetic data to train or fine-tune large language models? Focus on data quality, bias, and evaluation.

## Summary

Synthetic data can make LLM training and fine-tuning cheaper, faster, more scalable, and better targeted to rare or underrepresented cases. However, its value is highly conditional: unfiltered or recursively recycled outputs can narrow the data distribution, reproduce generator bias, weaken robustness, and create misleading evaluation results. The accumulated evidence supports using synthetic data as a controlled supplement to real or externally grounded data, with source diversity, deduplication, quality filtering, human or expert validation, and deployment-relevant evaluation.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Synthetic data provides practical benefits by expanding scarce training resources, reducing annotation and collection costs, supporting privacy-sensitive workflows, and targeting rare, edge-case, or underrepresented examples.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The benefits are consistently reported across several sources, but much of the evidence is practitioner, tutorial, or industry material rather than independent head-to-head studies.

**Evidence**

- Sources describe synthetic data as useful for augmenting limited labeled datasets, reducing collection and labeling effort, supporting privacy-sensitive applications, addressing class imbalance, and generating rare or difficult scenarios. [S1] [S3] [S4] [S9]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Synthetic data is generally safer and more useful as a supplement to real or externally grounded data than as an unverified replacement for it.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

This conclusion is supported by convergent evidence and reconciles both optimistic and cautionary findings, although the sources do not establish a universally safe synthetic-to-real ratio.

**Evidence**

- Multiple sources associate purely recursive or replacement-based synthetic training with distribution narrowing and degradation, while recommending real-data mixing, diverse generation sources, deduplication, filtering, and external validation. [S1] [S2] [S5] [S7] [S9]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

The main data-quality risk is distributional distortion: repeated, single-source, or weakly filtered synthetic data can reduce diversity, omit low-probability valid cases, and cause model collapse or task-specific regressions.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The mechanism is supported by multiple sources, including an empirical fine-tuning study and a specialized task comparison. The exact severity depends on the model, task, data mixture, and recycling regime.

**Evidence**

- Sources describe recursive synthetic training as narrowing the output distribution and report that source diversity, real-data retention, deduplication, and quality filtering mitigate collapse. A translation study found that aggregate translation quality could remain stable while markup-transfer capability declined. [S2] [S5] [S7] [S9] [S19]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Synthetic-data diversity and curation are important determinants of quality; volume alone does not ensure useful training data.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The direction of the finding is well supported, but the retrieved evidence does not establish general thresholds for source diversity or quality filtering.

**Evidence**

- An empirical study reports that higher diversity among synthetic-data sources mitigated distribution collapse and preserved broader linguistic and output diversity. Other sources emphasize curation, filtering, deduplication, task alignment, and human review. [S2] [S7] [S9] [S22]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Synthetic data does not automatically reduce bias and can reproduce stereotypes, demographic distortions, incorrect associations, or unrealistic subgroup examples.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The presence of bias in generated content is directly measured in a peer-reviewed study and reinforced by separate fairness-oriented evidence. The downstream effect on LLM fine-tuning remains less directly established.

**Evidence**

- A Scientific Reports study found significant stereotype and demographic-distribution biases in profiles generated by all four examined LLMs. Other sources identify bias amplification and underrepresentation as risks and emphasize that generated examples require fairness and semantic validation. [S3] [S4] [S10] [S14] [S15]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Targeted synthetic generation may improve subgroup coverage or downstream fairness, but nominal balancing alone can reduce utility or fail to improve real-world fairness when the synthetic distribution differs from deployment data.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The conditional nature of fairness gains is well supported, but the most direct controlled results concern vision or tabular models rather than LLM fine-tuning specifically.

**Evidence**

- Fairness-oriented sources describe audit-diagnose-generate-validate-retrain-re-audit workflows and report possible fairness gains from controlled generation. Other evidence identifies domain and bias gaps, fairness–utility tradeoffs, and substantially better measured fairness on artificially balanced tests than on realistic imbalanced distributions. [S10] [S13] [S15]

### Finding 7

**Claim**

Synthetic fine-tuning can preserve ordinary output quality while weakening adversarial robustness or safety safeguards.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The safety finding is directly reported but primarily comes from one study whose exact setup and external validity are only partially available.

**Evidence**

- The synthetic-diversity study reports reduced adversarial robustness despite preserved output quality and examines accidental weakening of safety safeguards as a separate downstream effect. [S7]
- Other sources caution that static benchmark success may not predict behavior in adversarial, tool-using, or realistic workflows. [S3] [S5]

### Finding 8

**Claim**

Synthetic evaluation data can scale testing and support controlled or relative comparisons, but it can systematically bias absolute performance estimates and should not be the sole basis for deployment decisions.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Evaluation bias is empirically supported in an IR setting, but its magnitude and direction across other LLM evaluation domains remain uncertain.

**Evidence**

- An empirical information-retrieval study found that synthetic queries differed systematically from human queries and that GPT-4 relevance judgments were approximately 0.28 points higher on average than human judgments. The resulting bias was more consequential for absolute performance estimates than for some relative rankings. [S16]
- Other sources warn that synthetic or static benchmarks can be contaminated, saturated, overfit, or weakly predictive of realistic tool-use, agentic, safety, and workflow behavior. [S3] [S4] [S5] [S8]

### Finding 9

**Claim**

Evaluation should be multidimensional and deployment-relevant rather than limited to aggregate benchmark accuracy.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple sources converge on multidimensional evaluation, although no universally validated protocol was retrieved.

**Evidence**

- The sources recommend comparison with human or trusted ground truth, plausibility and consistency checks, realistic population distributions, subgroup slices, edge and adversarial cases, and downstream outcome validation. [S3] [S4] [S12] [S13]
- Uncertainty-aware fairness measures can reveal unequal confidence or confidence in incorrect predictions that accuracy-only metrics miss. [S17]
- Task-specific measures are necessary because overall quality can remain stable while a targeted behavior, such as markup transfer or adversarial robustness, deteriorates. [S7] [S19]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- Some sources present synthetic data as highly effective at scale, particularly for instruction data and curated distillation, while others document collapse, narrow generalization, bias, or safety degradation. The difference appears conditional on source diversity, filtering, real-data retention, task, and whether outputs are recursively recycled. [S2] [S5] [S7] [S9] [S18]
- Targeted synthetic generation is presented as a possible fairness intervention, but balanced or synthetic tests can exaggerate fairness and domain gaps can reduce utility. Fairness claims therefore require realistic distributions and comparison with strong baselines. [S10] [S13] [S15]
- Synthetic evaluation may preserve relative system rankings in one IR study while still biasing absolute estimates and failing to predict deployment reliability. That result should not be generalized to safety-critical or agentic evaluation. [S5] [S16]
- Human data is less scalable and more expensive, but remains important for ground truth, realistic user behavior, frontier-capability evaluation, and independent validation. This is an operational tradeoff rather than a contradiction. [S4] [S9] [S12]
- The retrieved sources do not establish reliable synthetic-to-real mixing ratios, universal privacy guarantees, or the extent to which generator bias transfers into downstream demographic, cultural, or linguistic disparities after LLM fine-tuning. [S2] [S7] [S14] [S20]

## Remaining Gaps

- Independent controlled comparisons of human-authored versus synthetic instruction, preference, and safety data for LLMs on realistic deployment distributions.
- Quantitative guidance on source diversity, synthetic-to-real mixing ratios, and required levels of human review across LLM tasks and model sizes.
- Direct measurement of how generator stereotypes and demographic-distribution biases affect downstream LLM fairness after fine-tuning.
- Evidence on whether synthetic evaluation sets overestimate safety, factuality, calibration, tool-use, and agentic-task performance.
- Validated privacy and re-identification tests for synthetic text used in place of sensitive real data.
- Evaluation methods that reliably detect capability-specific regressions when aggregate task scores remain stable.

## Conclusion

Synthetic data is neither inherently safe nor inherently harmful. Its strongest real-world value is targeted augmentation: it can expand scarce data, reduce production costs, and create controlled examples for rare or underrepresented cases. Its principal risks arise when generated data is treated as ground truth—especially when outputs come from one model, are repetitive or weakly filtered, replace rather than supplement real data, or are evaluated only on synthetic or balanced benchmarks. A defensible practice is therefore to retain a trusted human or real-data anchor; use diverse, deduplicated, quality-filtered and externally grounded generation; audit demographic and semantic bias; and evaluate on held-out, realistic, subgroup-specific, adversarial, uncertainty-aware, and workflow-level tests. The evidence supports this controlled hybrid approach, but does not support universal claims about safe mixing ratios or guaranteed fairness and deployment validity.
