# Research Report

## Research Question

To what extent do social-media recommendation algorithms contribute to political polarization? Separate evidence that algorithms change what users see from evidence that they actually change political beliefs or behavior, and explain why the literature reaches different conclusions.

## Summary

The evidence review indicates that social-media recommendation algorithms influence political polarization primarily through content exposure and user behavior, leading to both significant support for claims of polarization and some counterclaims regarding the extent of their influence on individual beliefs. Several studies demonstrate the algorithms' role in amplifying like-minded content, leading to increased polarization; however, there is conflicting evidence indicating that these algorithms largely reinforce pre-existing user beliefs.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Social-media recommendation algorithms amplify political polarization by exposing users to like-minded content.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence reinforces existing claims about algorithms amplifying political polarization through creating echo chambers and limiting viewpoint diversity.

**Evidence**

- Exposure to like-minded arguments increases attitude polarization more than exposure to opposing arguments. Algorithmic selection may amplify polarization effects slightly. [S1]
- Social media algorithms rank and recommend content based on user behavior, shaping political visibility and engagement patterns that amplify polarization. [S3]
- A study found that algorithms directly alter users' political attitudes, supporting the claim that they significantly influence polarization. [S4]
- Social media algorithms create informational echo chambers, contributing to increasing political polarity and user distrust in media and government. [S6]
- Research suggests algorithms can significantly shift political feelings, linking closely to the assertion regarding polarization. [S8]
- Evidence suggests that algorithms amplify right-wing content against users' preferences, further indicating their role in increasing polarization. [S13]
- Algorithms amplify hyper-partisan news, often steering users toward polarized content, which aligns with evidence of increasing political polarization. [S15]
- Algorithms primarily show political news based on one's existing beliefs, pushing users into filter bubbles or echo chambers, contributing to confirmation bias and polarization. [S17]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Social-media algorithms primarily reinforce pre-existing social drivers rather than independently altering political beliefs.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence aligns with claims that algorithms mainly reflect existing biases in social media usage, reinforcing the understanding of social influence in algorithmic design.

**Evidence**

- Algorithms are influenced by social behaviors and existing preferences, making them more a reflection of societal conditions than independent agents driving change. [S2]
- The article highlights the critical role of social behavior and existing polarization in defining how users interact with algorithmically determined content, stressing that algorithms function within existing social contexts. [S3]
- Algorithms function within existing social contexts, often reflecting rather than altering political beliefs, reinforcing pre-existing biases. [S6]
- The framework discusses how social media algorithms may shape users' attitudes towards societal issues, suggesting that they may operate within rather than change existing beliefs, aligning with the claim of algorithms as reflections of social behaviors. [S16]
- The tendency of algorithms to predominantly expose users to partisan political news reflects pre-existing biases, suggesting that algorithms primarily reinforce social influences rather than instigate change. [S17]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Social media algorithms can shift political feelings significantly within a short period, revealing their strong influence on polarization.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence reinforces the claim that algorithms can rapidly influence political sentiment, corroborating prior findings that algorithms shift political feelings significantly within short periods.

**Evidence**

- Research demonstrated that social media algorithms can shift users' partisan feelings by a measurable amount within a week, which is usually seen over three years. [S8]
- Algorithms personalize content delivery by enhancing visibility of hyper-partisan narratives, indicating a significant effect on users' political feelings. [S15]
- The algorithmic customization of political narratives contributes to significant shifts in user sentiment, indicating that algorithms can rapidly affect political feelings. [S17]
- The algorithm significantly shifts users' political attitudes, indicating the capacity for rapid influence on polarization. [S18]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Social media algorithms create echo chambers that reinforce polarization by limiting exposure to diverse political views.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence supports the notion that algorithms contribute to echo chambers and polarization by tailoring user experiences to existing predispositions.

**Evidence**

- Algorithms contribute to echo chambers, resulting in significant political divides as users lose the ability to converse with those of different opinions. [S6]
- The algorithm helps in reinforcing existing political attitudes by adding a layer of personalization that limits exposure to diverse views. [S18]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Chronological feeds do not significantly alter polarization or key political attitudes, despite changes in content exposure.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The evidence provides strong empirical data showing no significant changes in political outcomes from altering content exposure methods.

**Evidence**

- A controlled study found that switching to chronological feeds did not affect levels of polarization or political attitudes over three months. [S12]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- Studies indicate that changing algorithms alone did not significantly alter users' political attitudes or polarization levels; users selectively engage with content that aligns with their beliefs. [S5]
- The new evidence indicates that users' preferences are often overridden, suggesting that algorithms are independent agents in content amplification rather than mere reflections of existing beliefs. [S13]

## Remaining Gaps

- There is a need to explore the mechanisms through which recommendation algorithms influence user exposure and political beliefs.
- Further investigation is needed to evaluate the long-term impacts of diverse ranking algorithms on users' beliefs and behaviors.
- Understanding the mechanisms behind how different social media algorithms affect political beliefs remains unresolved.
- There are unanswered questions regarding the long-term effects of algorithmic personalization on users' political beliefs and discourse.
- The effectiveness of algorithmic recommendations in counteracting polarization through diverse viewpoint exposure remains unresolved.

## Conclusion

The evidence reveals a complex relationship between social media recommendation algorithms and political polarization. On one hand, algorithms significantly influence the content users encounter, reinforcing polarization through echo chambers. On the other hand, they largely reflect pre-existing beliefs, with conflicting evidence regarding their independent capacity to alter political attitudes.
