# Research Report

## Research Question

What caused the Late Bronze Age collapse? Compare the evidence for climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, political instability, and systemic collapse, and distinguish well-supported explanations from speculation.

## Summary

The Late Bronze Age collapse (circa 1200 BCE) was likely caused by a combination of severe climate change, invasions, political instability, trade disruptions, and agricultural failures. Each factor interacts with the others, complicating the overall narrative of the collapse.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Climate change played a significant role in the Late Bronze Age collapse by causing severe droughts, leading to agricultural failures and famines.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Significant new direct evidence reinforces the critical role of climate change and drought in the collapse.

**Evidence**

- Discusses the impact of climate change, including prolonged drought and changing rainfall patterns, as a major factor in the collapse. [S3]
- Highlights how climate change led to crop failures and social unrest, contributing to the collapse of Bronze Age civilizations. [S4]
- Identifies climate change as a significant factor leading to drought that contributed to the Bronze Age crisis. [S10]
- Highlights the impact of climate change and severe drought, directly contributing to agricultural crisis and famine. [S13]
- Analyzes climate change effects such as natural disasters and food production crisis as significant to the collapse. [S16]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Invasions by the Sea Peoples contributed to the collapse of Bronze Age civilizations.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

New direct evidence elaborates on the Sea Peoples' role in destabilizing trade routes, which is crucial to the collapse narrative.

**Evidence**

- Describes the Sea Peoples as a key factor contributing to the invasions and subsequent collapse. [S4]
- Explains the role of the Sea Peoples' invasions in disrupting trade and contributing to the collapse of Bronze Age civilizations, hinting at their migration being influenced by climate change. [S19]
- Highlights the Sea Peoples as a coalition that disrupted trade routes and destroyed coastal cities, contributing significantly to the Bronze Age collapse. [S26]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Political instability and internal rebellions were significant factors in the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Additional direct evidence reinforces the significance of political instability but doesn't add overwhelming support.

**Evidence**

- Indicates that internal rebellions and class wars played a role in the societal upheaval leading to collapse. [S2]
- Discusses internal disruptions, including class wars and political instability, as key elements of the collapse. [S4]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Trade disruption contributed to the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Direct evidence from the new source enhances understanding of the prolonged effects of trade disruption leading to the collapse.

**Evidence**

- Highlights how disruptions in trade networks resulted in economic downturns, contributing to the collapse. [S5]
- Indicates that disruptions in trade due to climatic changes and invasions significantly affected social stability, reinforcing the importance of trade in the Late Bronze Age collapse narrative. [S19]
- Describes the critical role of trade disruptions in the collapse of Bronze Age societies due to dependencies on intercontinental trade networks. [S27]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Severe droughts leading to agricultural failures and famine were a significant factor in the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Further corroboration of direct evidence linking drought to agriculture and famine solidifies the claim.

**Evidence**

- Discusses archaeological and palaeo-climatic evidence of severe droughts leading to failed harvests and famine. [S13]
- Links agricultural failures due to climate shifts to the broader socio-economic crises during the Late Bronze Age. [S17]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Warfare and invasions played a dramatic role in the destruction of civilizations during the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While direct evidence reinforces the warfare aspect, the complexity and multifaceted nature of the collapse narrative still require further support.

**Evidence**

- Details the invasions by the Sea Peoples and their destructive impact on various cities in the region. [S13]
- Describes the destructive role of the Sea Peoples in the collapse of Bronze Age societies, establishing an integral connection to warfare and invasions in the overall narrative of collapse. [S19]

### Finding 7

**Claim**

Internal rebellions and political instability exacerbated the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Indirect evidence supports the notion of internal instability influenced by external pressures but remains inconclusive in specific impacts.

**Evidence**

- Discusses internal strife and its role in destabilizing polities during the Late Bronze Age. [S13]
- Reinforces the theme of internal strife and its connection to the broader societal crises, exacerbated by environmental and external challenges during the period. [S20]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- No material conflict was identified in the retrieved evidence.

## Remaining Gaps

- No major remaining gap was identified within the research scope.

## Conclusion

The Late Bronze Age collapse resulted from an interplay of contributing factors, notably climate change, invasions by the Sea Peoples, political instability, trade disruptions, and agricultural failures. Each factor not only acted independently but also influenced others, creating a complex web of causes that ultimately led to the widespread collapse of societies during this period.
