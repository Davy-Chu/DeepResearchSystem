## Comprehensiveness

### R1

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.75
- Rationale: The report makes an important and well-supported distinction between nameplate/process capture and facility-level abatement, and it includes a concrete operating-period example. It falls short of a fuller evaluation because actual stored quantities, availability metrics, and net emissions reductions are not systematically demonstrated.
- Candidate evidence:
  - Petra Nova is reported to have captured approximately 3.54 million tonnes during a three-year demonstration period.
  - The report distinguishes a 92.4% capture rate for the treated stream from treatment of only about 37% of the host unit's emissions, or roughly 33% of total Unit 8 emissions.
  - It discusses downtime involving the capture plant, host unit, pipeline, and EOR field, and notes that commercial-scale power-sector experience is limited.
  - It explicitly states that the project evidence does not establish whole-plant net emissions reductions or full lifecycle climate benefit.
- Missing:
  - It does not provide measured, independently verified injection and permanent-storage quantities across representative projects.
  - Operating availability, cumulative uptime, and project-wide capture performance are not quantified systematically beyond discussion of Petra Nova downtime.
  - The report mentions high-performing projects such as Sleipner and Quest but supplies little measured evidence for their actual stored quantities, retention, or operating periods.

### R2

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.50
- Rationale: The report covers the main integrity topics and correctly flags the distinction between storage claims and genuine climate mitigation. However, it largely describes uncertainties and governance requirements rather than assessing demonstrated permanence and safety with measured evidence.
- Candidate evidence:
  - Petra Nova is described as having transported CO₂ through an 81-mile pipeline and injected it for EOR, with construction, operation, transport, injection, and monitoring documented.
  - The report identifies saline-aquifer storage, geological uncertainty, permanence, leakage, safety, environmental impacts, monitoring, verification, and long-term public liability as relevant issues.
  - It explains that EOR complicates climate accounting and notes that lifecycle studies produce different results depending on whether downstream oil emissions are included.
  - It states that announced or nominal storage capacity is not equivalent to independently verified permanent storage.
- Missing:
  - There is no measured evidence of long-term retention, migration behavior, or leakage rates from geological storage projects.
  - Transport and injection safety are discussed mainly as requirements and risks rather than evaluated using incident, integrity, or monitoring performance data.
  - Long-term responsibility and liability are identified but not assessed across actual regulatory regimes.
  - The report does not clearly distinguish demonstrated permanent saline storage from EOR-associated injection using project-level climate-accounting evidence.

### R3

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.75

- Candidate evidence:
  - It gives an indicative energy-linked cost range of approximately $50–600 per tonne of CO₂ and emphasizes application-specific variation.

  - The report does not provide a consistent comparison with credible alternatives such as electrification, renewables, efficiency, or process changes using comparable cost assumptions.
