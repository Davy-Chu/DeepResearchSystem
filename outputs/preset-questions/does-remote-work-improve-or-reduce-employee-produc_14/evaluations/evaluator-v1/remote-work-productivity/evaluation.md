#### F2: SUPPORTED

- Claim: Hybrid work arrangements optimize productivity and employee retention.
- Sources: S1, S3, S23
- Rationale: The cited sources directly support the claim’s two key elements: hybrid work maintains or improves productivity and reduces turnover. S1 reports no measurable productivity difference between hybrid and fully in-office workers alongside lower attrition; S3 describes structured hybrid work as balancing steady productivity with reduced quit rates; and S23 states that hybrid work supports productivity and helps attract and retain talent. The word “optimize” is supported in the sources as an evaluative conclusion, though the evidence is framed primarily around maintaining productivity while improving retention rather than maximizing both outcomes.
- Supporting text: S1: Hybrid workers showed no measurable productivity difference from fully in-office peers and had 35% lower attrition; the source calls this an organizational win. S3: Structured hybrid work provides an optimal balance of steady productivity and high employee retention, with quit rates reduced by 33%. S23: Organizations aligning with hybrid preferences are better positioned to attract and retain talent, while productivity depends on effective systems and technology.

S6 reports that fully remote workers were 10% less productive than in-office workers on collaborative tasks and had difficulty accessing informal help from colleagues.
