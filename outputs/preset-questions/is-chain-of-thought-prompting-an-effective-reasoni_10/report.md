# Research Report

## Research Question

Is chain-of-thought prompting an effective reasoning strategy for LLMs, or does it primarily improve output formatting? The literature disagrees—find the real fault lines and explain what accounts for the conflicting results.

## Summary

The literature is not best characterized as a binary dispute between “CoT works” and “CoT is only formatting.” The strongest synthesis is conditional: CoT can improve final-answer accuracy by providing a useful test-time scaffold, particularly for earlier or non-reasoning models and difficult multi-step tasks. However, visible rationales are not reliable evidence of faithful internal reasoning, and few-shot CoT exemplars often add little beyond output-format alignment for strong recent models that already respond effectively to zero-shot step-by-step instructions. Conflicting findings mainly arise from differences in model generation and capability, the baseline used, task and difficulty, prompt/scaffold design, reasoning length, evaluation and answer parsing, and whether success is measured by final accuracy or independently verified reasoning.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

CoT is an effective conditional intervention for final-answer performance, especially in some earlier-model and difficult multi-step settings.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The classic result is directly reported by the original study or a Google Research account, and S13 provides additional comparative evidence. However, the studies use different model generations and do not establish a universal effect.

**Evidence**

- The original CoT work reports substantial gains over standard direct prompting on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning tasks, with benefits emerging at sufficiently large model scales; PaLM 540B achieved a large improvement on GSM8K over standard prompting. [S20] [S27]
- A cross-model, cross-dataset study reports generally robust zero-shot CoT gains across six models and six question-answering datasets, including scientific, medical, knowledge-based, and logical reasoning tasks. [S13]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

For strong recent models, the incremental value of few-shot CoT exemplars may be primarily output-format alignment rather than additional reasoning.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

S25 directly addresses the central claim, but the accumulated excerpt does not include complete tables or enough detail to determine how fully formatting, length, token budget, sampling, and parsing were controlled.

**Evidence**

- A primary 2025 study on GSM8K and MATH reports that traditional and enhanced few-shot CoT exemplars did not improve recent strong models over zero-shot CoT, and identifies output-format alignment as their primary function. [S25]
- The study reports that strong models focused more on instructions and target questions than on exemplar content; even exemplars generated by advanced models failed to improve reasoning performance. [S25]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

The classic positive result and the newer formatting result use different baselines, which explains much of their apparent contradiction.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The difference in comparison conditions is explicit across the sources and provides a coherent explanation without requiring either result to be rejected.

**Evidence**

- The original work compared few-shot CoT with ordinary direct or standard few-shot prompting on earlier large models. [S20] [S27]
- The newer study compares few-shot CoT with zero-shot CoT on stronger recent models, where the step-by-step response policy may already be available without exemplars. [S25]
- A separate repeated-trial evaluation finds modest gains for some non-reasoning models but marginal or negative incremental effects for reasoning-native models. [S5]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Final-answer accuracy and faithful reasoning are distinct outcomes; a correct answer does not establish that the displayed CoT caused the answer or accurately represents the model’s computation.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple sources directly make or support the distinction between output accuracy and causal or step-level faithfulness.

**Evidence**

- Faithfulness tests based on early answering and injected mistakes found substantial task variation and little correlation between faithfulness and CoT accuracy gains. Some tasks changed answers little when the chain was truncated, while others depended more on continued reasoning. [S8]
- The REVEAL discussion explicitly distinguishes correct final answers from logically correct, relevant, and evidence-supported reasoning steps. [S10]
- The original CoT presentation itself describes generated chains as mimicking a thought process and does not establish that the neural network is actually reasoning in the human or mechanistic sense. [S18] [S20]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

The evidence does not support the universal claim that CoT is merely formatting.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The mixed-mechanism conclusion is supported by positive performance results, formatting-specific findings, and the filler-token intervention. The exact causal contribution of semantic intermediate computation remains uncertain.

**Evidence**

- S25 supports a formatting explanation for few-shot exemplars in strong recent mathematical models, but S20 and S27 report large performance gains from CoT over direct prompting in earlier large models. [S25] [S27]
- Filler tokens alone did not improve accuracy in the summarized faithfulness experiments, suggesting that mere verbosity is insufficient; structured intermediate content may matter in at least some settings. [S8]
- Structured Hi-CoT reports higher accuracy and shorter traces than flat CoT on its tested mathematical benchmarks, indicating that the organization of intermediate steps can affect performance, although this result does not isolate semantic reasoning from other prompt effects. [S1]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Model capability and task difficulty are major moderators of CoT effectiveness.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The model-regime pattern is reported by both classic and recent evaluations, though the studies do not use identical models or protocols.

**Evidence**

- The original work describes CoT benefits as an emergent property of sufficiently large models and reports larger effects on challenging arithmetic tasks than on some commonsense tasks. [S20] [S27]
- Recent evaluations report gains for some non-reasoning models but small or negative incremental effects for reasoning-native models. [S5]
- The 2025 formatting study reports that weaker models may benefit from traditional exemplars while strong recent models do not show additional reasoning gains over zero-shot CoT. [S25]

### Finding 7

**Claim**

CoT length and structure create another fault line: more reasoning steps are not automatically better.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The evidence gives a plausible and experimentally supported moderator, but it is concentrated in recent mathematical preprints and does not establish how much of the effect is semantic reasoning versus token allocation or response policy.

**Evidence**

- A recent study reports an inverted-U relationship in which performance initially improves with longer CoT but later declines because of noise and accumulated intermediate errors. [S14]
- The study links optimal length to both task difficulty and model capability: harder tasks may need deeper decomposition, while stronger models can solve subtasks in fewer steps. [S14]
- Hi-CoT attributes improvements over flat CoT to adaptive planning, compression bottlenecks, reduced redundancy, and less plan-execution drift. [S1]

### Finding 8

**Claim**

Evaluation protocol can materially change the apparent value of CoT.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Both sources directly identify parsing or metric choices that can reverse or qualify comparisons.

**Evidence**

- The 2025 study reports that common GSM8K evaluators underestimated zero-shot CoT by mishandling formats such as boxed answers; correcting extraction often made zero-shot CoT comparable to or better than few-shot CoT. [S25]
- Repeated-trial GPQA evaluation found that conclusions differed across average accuracy, majority correctness, perfect accuracy, and variability, while one-shot testing could hide instability. [S5]

### Finding 9

**Claim**

“CoT” denotes several different interventions, not one uniform treatment.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The intervention differences are directly described, but the retrieved evidence does not provide a unified causal comparison among them.

**Evidence**

- The sources distinguish few-shot and zero-shot CoT from self-consistency, contrastive prompting, hierarchical planning, Tree of Thoughts, least-to-most decomposition, latent CoT, and other structured variants. [S6] [S9] [S12]
- These variants differ in whether they add demonstrations, decomposition, search, aggregation, retrieval, or formatting constraints, so their effects should not be pooled as if they tested the same mechanism. [S1] [S6] [S9]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- The original literature reports striking CoT gains, while the newer study reports that few-shot exemplars mainly align format for strong recent models. This is largely a baseline and model-generation conflict: classic studies compare against direct prompting on earlier models; newer work compares against already-effective zero-shot CoT on stronger models. [S25] [S27]
- Some sources describe CoT as transparent reasoning, while faithfulness studies show that explanations can be post-hoc, incorrect, or weakly related to the accuracy gain. “Interpretability” in the practical sense of displaying steps should therefore not be equated with faithful access to internal computation. [S8] [S9] [S10] [S20]
- The evidence that CoT adds useful computation is mixed. Filler tokens alone did not improve accuracy, but semantically structured CoT can improve performance in some regimes. The available material does not isolate semantic computation from all response-policy and formatting effects. [S8] [S12] [S20]
- Positive cross-domain evidence exists, including scientific, medical, and logical QA, while other summarized work suggests the largest benefits concentrate in mathematical and symbolic reasoning. The retrieved material does not provide matched protocols sufficient to reconcile these claims quantitatively. [S13] [S28]
- Claims about hierarchical or length-calibrated CoT are promising but come primarily from recent preprints and are not independently replicated in the retrieved material. [S1] [S14]
- S11 appears relevant to multidimensional evaluation of generated reasoning traces, but the retrieved excerpt contains no usable methods, results, or conclusions. [S11]

## Remaining Gaps

- Whether zero-shot CoT itself improves reasoning over a format-matched direct-answer or hidden-reasoning condition when output format, response length, token budget, sampling, and parsing are held constant.
- Whether the formatting findings from S25 generalize beyond Qwen2.5 and GSM8K/MATH to proprietary frontier models, reasoning-native systems, and non-mathematical tasks.
- How much of the classic CoT gains survive corrected answer extraction and matched-format controls.
- Whether accuracy-improving chains are independently verified as logically correct and causally involved in answer selection.
- How model capability, task difficulty, chain length, semantic content, and aggregation methods interact under a common experimental protocol.
- Whether claims about CoT’s brittleness under distribution shift and concentration of benefits in math or symbolic tasks replicate in the underlying studies summarized by S28.

## Conclusion

CoT should be treated as a conditional test-time scaffold, not as a universally effective reasoning strategy and not as a reliable readout of latent reasoning. It clearly can improve final answers relative to direct prompting in some earlier-model and difficult multi-step settings. But for strong recent models, especially when the relevant comparison is few-shot CoT versus zero-shot CoT, exemplars may primarily standardize output format because the model already possesses the necessary step-by-step response policy. The literature’s real fault lines are therefore model regime, baseline, task and difficulty, prompt variant, chain length, evaluation protocol, and the definition of success. CoT is best evaluated empirically for the target model and task, with format- and token-matched controls and separate measures of final accuracy, step-level validity, and faithfulness.
