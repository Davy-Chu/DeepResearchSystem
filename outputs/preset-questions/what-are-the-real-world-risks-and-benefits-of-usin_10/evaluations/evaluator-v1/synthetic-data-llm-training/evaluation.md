- Coverage: 0.50
- Depth: 0.50
- Rationale: The report recognizes mixed versus synthetic-heavy training and focuses on LLM-generated fine-tuning, but it does not provide the requested structured scope distinctions or explain all of why they matter.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report distinguishes synthetic data used for “task-specific fine-tuning” and repeatedly discusses “synthetic fine-tuning.”
  - It recommends a “hybrid, quality-controlled workflow” and says to “retain human or real-data anchors.”
  - It discusses “recursive or single-source synthetic training” and contrasts “entirely synthetic data” with retaining real data.
- Missing:
  - It does not clearly define or compare synthetic data generated by LLMs versus programs or other generators.
  - Pretraining is not substantively distinguished from fine-tuning; the report is overwhelmingly about fine-tuning.

- Depth: 0.75

- Candidate evidence:

### R8

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.75

- Candidate evidence:

  - It repeatedly states that benefits are conditional and that outcomes depend on generation controls, distributional alignment, model adaptation, subgroup validation, task, and experimental setting.
  - It recommends retaining real data, diversifying sources, validating and filtering records, preserving lineage, and auditing quality, fairness, uncertainty, calibration, robustness, safety, and privacy.

  - The practical safeguards are clear, but evidence-based deployment gates are not fully specified.

### Novel Value

- The report usefully connects synthetic-data risks to privacy leakage, including specific reported increases in PII extraction and membership-inference performance after synthetic-data fine-tuning.
- It highlights the important distinction between dataset-level balance or fairness metrics and demonstrated fairness of the trained model on independent real-world data.
- It synthesizes a practical hybrid workflow combining real-data anchors, source diversity, validation, lineage tracking, and repeated subgroup and robustness audits.
- It presents model-collapse and privacy claims conditionally rather than treating synthetic data as inherently private or collapse as inevitable.
