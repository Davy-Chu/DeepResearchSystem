# Research Report

## Research Question

How close are quantum computers to providing commercially useful advantages over classical computers? Separate demonstrated quantum advantage from theoretical or projected advantages, and identify the biggest remaining technical barriers.

## Summary

This report synthesizes findings on the current state of quantum computing, assessing demonstrated advantages over classical computers, potential transformative impacts, and significant barriers to widespread commercial use.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

IBM researchers demonstrated quantum advantage over classical computers in a real-world experiment, with a success rate of 93% in a specific task, surpassing classical systems.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The existing evidence continues to support the demonstrated quantum advantages previously claimed.

**Evidence**

- IBM researchers claim to have demonstrated quantum advantage in a practical scenario. [S1]
- IBM claims to have proven quantum advantage experimentally, showing better performance than classical computers. [S2]
- IBM researchers have shown quantum computers outperform classical systems in practical tasks, indicating a significant step towards quantum advantage. [S6]
- Google's proof of quantum supremacy highlights the feasibility of quantum computers achieving critical tasks beyond classical capabilities. [S7]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Quantum computers have the potential to revolutionize various fields by solving complex problems more efficiently than classical computers.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Evidence consistency from new sources reinforces the potential for quantum computing advancements to significantly impact various fields.

**Evidence**

- Quantum computing can solve problems exponentially faster than classical systems. [S3]
- Quantum computers offer advantages in optimization, cryptography, and simulation tasks. [S4]
- Quantum computers are believed to be millions of times more powerful than classical computers. [S5]
- Predictions indicate that quantum computers will achieve practical advantages in numerous fields by 2026, providing early commercial applications in areas like pharmaceuticals and finance. [S16]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

The existing quantum computing technology is not yet able to effectively solve complex problems at large scales due to noise and scalability issues.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Additional evidence supports the claim that noise and scalability issues are significant barriers that remain to be fully addressed in practical quantum computing.

**Evidence**

- Current quantum computers are too noisy to achieve perfect results. [S1]
- Developing a robust and scalable quantum computer poses substantial challenges. [S5]
- Quantum computing still faces significant technical challenges like noise and scalability, which hinder effective problem-solving at large scales. [S6]
- Key challenges in commercial quantum computing include decoherence, error rates, and noise mitigation, affecting scalability and stability. [S10]
- OQC's research on dual-rail dimon qubit technology is a breakthrough in error detection and addresses scalability issues, bringing quantum computing closer to commercial viability. [S11]
- The DISCOVERY project in the UK aims to overcome technological barriers to commercial quantum computing, focusing on methods like ion trap and optical qubits. [S12]
- Current quantum computers face challenges such as scaling and qubit management which hinder commercial viability, but advancements in qubit technology suggest progress is being made. [S13]
- OQC's advancements in quantum error correction are critical for unlocking commercial potential, aiming for a viable quantum computer by 2028. [S14]
- Error correction breakthroughs are enabling reliable quantum computing, addressing noise issues effectively, and bringing practical systems closer to reality. [S16]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

In 2026, significant breakthroughs in quantum error correction and fault tolerance have advanced the maturation of large-scale practical quantum computing systems, shifting from the NISQ era to fault-tolerant quantum computing.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The additional evidence strongly reinforces previous assertions about progress in quantum error correction and its implications for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

**Evidence**

- Breakthroughs in error correction and fault tolerance have improved the operational efficiency of quantum computers, marking a shift to practical applications. [S25]
- Scalable quantum error correction is crucial for future utility-scale quantum applications, indicating significant advancements in error correction techniques. [S26]
- Quantinuum's achievement of a fully fault-tolerant universal gate set lays the foundation for scalable quantum computing, representing a critical technical milestone. [S27]
- The developments in quantum error correction codes have transitioned from theory to practical implementation, critical for achieving fault-tolerant quantum computing. [S28]
- Recent progress in quantum error correction supports the transition from theoretical concepts to practical implementations in fault-tolerant quantum computing. [S30]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Achieving scalable and reliable fault-tolerant quantum computers remains a major technical hurdle, particularly addressing long-term stable operation of error correction systems and large-scale qubit integration.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The strengthened evidence base reiterates the existing claim regarding the complexity of achieving scalable and reliable fault-tolerant quantum computers.

**Evidence**

- Despite advancements, the engineering difficulty for large-scale fault-tolerant quantum devices still poses significant challenges. [S25]
- Quantinuum highlights that a reliable architecture is essential for true industrial-scale applications, which includes overcoming notable engineering challenges. [S27]
- Current technological hurdles in quantum computing include the engineering difficulties tied to implementing scalable fault-tolerant systems. [S28]
- IBM highlights the engineering difficulties tied to implementing scalable fault-tolerant quantum systems in a large-scale environment. [S41]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Current and projected technological advancements position quantum computing to transition from theoretical capabilities to practical implementations with commercial applications emerging by 2026.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

New innovations in quantum hardware further highlight progress towards practical implementations of quantum computing by 2026, alongside parallel development of effective qubit management strategies.

**Evidence**

- Advancements in quantum error correction codes represent critical steps towards large-scale fault-tolerant systems. [S36]
- Research emphasizes the importance of qubit scalability and developing fault-tolerant quantum computing systems for maintaining the integrity of quantum information. [S35]
- Developments in logical qubits demonstrate progress towards fault-tolerant architectures needed for large-scale quantum computing. [S43]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- No material conflict was identified in the retrieved evidence.

## Remaining Gaps

- No major remaining gap was identified within the research scope.

## Conclusion

Quantum computing has demonstrated tangible advantages over classical computing in specific tasks; however, significant challenges persist regarding noise, scalability, and fault tolerance. Practical applications are projected to emerge as advancements in error correction and qubit management continue to evolve, with meaningful capabilities expected around 2026. Despite this positive trajectory, achieving a fully operational, scalable, and reliable quantum computing architecture remains a complex and ongoing challenge.
