# Research Report

## Research Question

What caused the Late Bronze Age collapse? Compare the evidence for climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, political instability, and systemic collapse, and distinguish well-supported explanations from speculation.

## Summary

The Late Bronze Age collapse remains a complex interplay of various factors. Climate change is supported by substantial evidence, particularly in relation to agricultural instability. However, it is essential to consider other contributors such as warfare, migration, and trade disruptions, which also played significant roles in the societal collapse.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Climate change may have contributed to the Late Bronze Age collapse, but its significance relative to warfare and trade disruptions is still debated.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Integrating diverse evidence confirms and strengthens the current claim regarding climate change's significant role in the Late Bronze Age collapse and enhances the overall context.

**Evidence**

- Evidence shows a correlation between severe climate conditions and agricultural failures around 1250 BCE, which contributed to destabilization. [S3]
- The analysis emphasizes climate change as a primary cause, affecting agricultural output and food security during the collapse. [S2]
- Dendrochronological evidence shows significant drought years during the Late Bronze Age, impacting crop yields and societal stability. [S5]
- Severe droughts associated with the collapse were intensified by natural climate cycles converging, leading to significant agricultural stress. [S10]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Significant climatic changes, particularly aridity, around the Late Bronze Age contributed to societal collapse by impacting agricultural productivity and population stability.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple sources, including paleoclimate proxies, indicate a clear correlation between increased aridity and declines in agricultural productivity, which in turn affected population stability.

**Evidence**

- Data indicates a sharp increase in Northern Hemisphere temperatures and a subsequent decrease during the abandonment period of Bronze-Age Palatial centers, coinciding with declines in agricultural production. [S6]
- Pollen-derived climatic proxy suggests drier conditions in Syria from the late 13th/early 12th centuries BC, likely affecting settlement patterns and agricultural practices. [S7]
- Research highlights significant climatic events and adaptations in agricultural practices that reflect societal responses to drying conditions during the Bronze Age. [S8]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

A combination of severe climatic changes, compounded by natural climate cycles, contributed to the Late Bronze Age collapse by exacerbating agricultural stresses, but this should be qualified by acknowledging the possible significant roles of warfare, migration, and economic disruption.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While there is support for climatic changes as instrumental, the evidence suggests that multiple factors collectively contributed to societal instability.

**Evidence**

- Research indicates that severe droughts associated with the collapse were intensified by natural climate cycles converging, leading to significant agricultural stress. [S10]
- Climate modeling shows that the Eastern Mediterranean experienced compounded drought conditions due to both long-term trends and short-term climate fluctuations. [S11]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

The Sea Peoples were responsible for societal destruction and instability during the Late Bronze Age, influenced by crises leading to migration and military incursions.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While there is ongoing recognition of the Sea Peoples' role, the new evidence does not significantly alter the previous interpretations of their context in relation to climatic stressors and societal conditions, highlighting the complexity of the factors involved.

**Evidence**

- The Sea Peoples caused significant destruction and decline in various civilizations during the Late Bronze Age, acting as marauding raiders contributing to societal collapses. [S21]
- Historical records illustrate the Sea Peoples as a coalition of groups responsible for widespread havoc across coastal cities, suggesting their role in the breakdown of societal structures. [S22]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- The extent to which climate change alone caused societal collapse without considering the impact of other factors like warfare and political instability is debated, indicating a need for a nuanced understanding. [S2] [S3]

## Remaining Gaps

- Subquestions SQ3, SQ4, SQ5, and SQ6 remain unresolved due to lack of specific evidence.

## Conclusion

The Late Bronze Age collapse resulted from a combination of factors including climate change, warfare, and economic instability, with ongoing debate about the relative contributions of these factors. Understanding the intricate interdependencies among these elements is crucial for a comprehensive view.
