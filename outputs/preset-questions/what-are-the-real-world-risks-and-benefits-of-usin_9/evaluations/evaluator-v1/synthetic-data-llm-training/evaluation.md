- Coverage: 0.50
- Depth: 0.50
- Rationale: The report recognizes mixed versus replacement training and discusses fine-tuning and recursive training, but the relevant settings are not defined comprehensively enough to establish their differences.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The summary recommends synthetic data as “curated augmentation rather than a wholesale replacement for human or real-world data.”
  - Finding 4 distinguishes “retaining real data” and additive mixtures from “replacement regimes.”
  - Finding 3 discusses “recursive, single-source, or predominantly synthetic training,” while Finding 6 addresses “synthetic fine-tuning.”
- Missing:

  - Pretraining is not explicitly discussed as a separate regime from fine-tuning.
  - It does not systematically explain how the generator type, pretraining versus fine-tuning, and pure-synthetic versus mixed training change the risks and benefits.

### R6

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.75

  - The remaining gaps explicitly call for independent methods to measure “demographic fairness effects.”
- Missing:

### R8

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.75
- Rationale: The conclusion is appropriately conditional, uncertainty-aware, and safeguard-oriented, but it remains more of a high-level recommendation than a fully specified evidence-based decision framework.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The conclusion says synthetic data is valuable particularly when human data is “scarce, expensive, sensitive, or unable to cover rare cases.”
  - It recommends “carefully curated, diverse augmentation alongside retained real or human data, with independent validation and deployment-oriented testing.”
  - The conflicts section says outcomes depend on whether data is “curated, diverse, externally checked” versus “recursive, single-source, or unverified.”
  - The report states that there are no universal benefits or harms and that no universal safe synthetic-data percentage has been established.
  - The remaining gaps identify unresolved effects of mixture proportions, source diversity, filtering, deduplication, model size, languages, tasks, privacy, and generalization.
- Missing:
  - Generator capability and target-model capability are not developed as explicit decision factors.
  - The report does not offer concrete deployment decision thresholds or a practical go/no-go checklist beyond general curation and evaluation safeguards.
  - The effects of prompting, training regime, and synthetic-to-real ratio are acknowledged but not analyzed in operational detail.

### Novel Value

- The report synthesizes a conditional position rather than a universal verdict: curated, diverse synthetic augmentation can help, while recursive, single-source, or predominantly synthetic training can narrow distributions and harm generalization.
- It usefully separates synthetic-dataset checks from downstream model evaluation and emphasizes independent held-out real-world evidence.
- It identifies unresolved practical questions, including safe synthetic-to-real mixtures, demographic fairness effects, privacy leakage, and deployment generalization.
