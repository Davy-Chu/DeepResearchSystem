## Coverage and Depth

### R1

- Coverage: 0.25
- Depth: 0.25
- Rationale: The report recognizes complexity but does not establish the basic chronological, geographic, and societal scope required before causal evaluation.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report calls the subject the “Late Bronze Age collapse” and repeatedly refers to “the period leading to the collapse.”
  - The summary describes it as a “complex event influenced by multiple factors.”
- Missing:
  - No chronological boundaries are given.
  - No geographic scope is defined beyond scattered references to the Hittite region and the Eastern Mediterranean.
  - The report does not explain whether the collapse was a single event or a regionally uneven sequence.
  - It does not distinguish the breakdown of palatial or imperial institutions from population disappearance, cultural extinction, or later continuity.

### R2

- Coverage: 0.75
- Depth: 0.50
- Rationale: Climate and subsistence evidence are mentioned and connected mechanistically, but the causal inference is too confident and lacks regional testing and proxy criticism.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report cites “tree ring analysis” showing severe droughts in the Hittite region coinciding with the collapse [S5].
  - It refers to “tree-ring analysis and pollen records” as evidence for drought, failed harvests, and famine in the Eastern Mediterranean [S9].
  - It links drought to agricultural failure, famine, and weakened social and political stability in Findings 1 and 5.
- Missing:
  - Regional variation and counterexamples are not assessed; the report does not identify areas that experienced environmental stress without collapse or collapse without documented drought.
  - It does not distinguish evidence for environmental change from evidence that environmental change caused political collapse in a sustained way.
  - Proxy dating, spatial limitations, uncertainty, and possible differences between local drought and basin-wide conditions are not discussed.
  - The high confidence assigned to climate causation is asserted rather than justified comparatively.

### R3

- Coverage: 0.50
- Depth: 0.50
- Rationale: Conflict and destruction are addressed, but the report largely assumes the causal interpretation it is supposed to evaluate and does not handle the evidentiary limitations surrounding the Sea Peoples.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report cites “attacks on urban centers that coincided with the timeline of the collapse” [S4].
  - It states that historical accounts document “invasions by the Sea Peoples” and links them to urban destruction [S2].
  - It also connects invasions and attacks with trade disruption and political fragmentation [S6, S9, S12].
- Missing:
  - The report treats “the Sea Peoples” and their “invasions” as a significant explanatory agent without discussing the ambiguity of the term or whether it denotes a unified coalition.
  - It does not distinguish destruction evidence from identification of attackers, attack scale, coordination, or precise chronology.
  - Destruction layers are not independently evaluated, and alternative explanations such as internal conflict, abandonment, fire, or reconstruction are not considered.
  - It does not test whether warfare explains both collapsed and surviving sites or whether conflict was a cause, consequence, or amplifier.

### R4

- Coverage: 0.50
- Depth: 0.25
- Rationale: Migration is named as a mechanism, but the treatment is largely generic and does not evaluate the directness or limits of mobility evidence.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report claims that “forced movements were linked to warfare and political instability” [S8].
  - It says migration patterns were driven by “environmental and political crises” and were integral to collapse dynamics [S10].
- Missing:
  - No concrete, direct evidence for particular migrating populations is presented.
  - The report does not distinguish migration evidence from inferences based on artifacts, cultural change, texts, or later traditions.
  - The timing, scale, geographic distribution, and direction of movements are unspecified.
  - It does not establish whether mobility preceded collapse, resulted from it, or fed back into conflict and instability.
  - Genetic, linguistic, archaeological, or settlement evidence and their limitations are absent.

### R5

- Coverage: 0.50
- Depth: 0.50
- Rationale: The report identifies plausible economic and political mechanisms and gives one textual example, but it does not adequately establish their chronology, independence, or causal direction.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report states that the “collapse of trade routes crippled economies reliant on imported materials” [S9].
  - It cites communications from Ugarit “appealing for aid amid impending disaster” as evidence of economic disruption [S9].
  - It refers to internal rebellions, “political failures in managing famine and social unrest,” and “governance issues” [S3, S4, S12].
- Missing:
  - Pre-crisis interdependence, tribute, provisioning, and resource-supply networks are not described in concrete terms.
  - The report does not distinguish trade disruption as a cause from a consequence of warfare, famine, or administrative collapse; indeed, Finding 6 mainly presents it as a consequence of invasions and unrest.
  - Fiscal pressure, dynastic conflict, administrative breakdown, and institutional vulnerabilities are asserted but not evidenced in detail.
  - Ugarit is used without explaining whether its experience generalizes to other polities.
  - Surviving or adapting economies and alternative trade routes are not considered.

### R6

- Coverage: 0.25
- Depth: 0.25
- Rationale: Confidence labels are not a substitute for evidence evaluation. The report provides little methodological scrutiny of the evidence underlying its causal claims.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report assigns confidence levels such as “High” and “Medium” to its findings.
  - It acknowledges in the migration finding that the evidence “requires further exploration” and in the trade finding that more evidence is needed.
- Missing:
  - Primary observations are not separated from secondary interpretations or broad claims.
  - The report does not assess the reliability or propagandistic character of historical texts, including accounts of invasions.
  - Archaeological incompleteness, destruction-recording bias, proxy uncertainty, small genetic samples, and model assumptions are not addressed.
  - Chronological precision, geographic scope, and independence among the cited studies are not evaluated.
  - Temporal coincidence is repeatedly used as support for causation without a systematic warning against that inference.
  - The source list includes general, popular, and potentially low-authority sources, but source quality is not critically assessed.

### R7

- Coverage: 0.25
- Depth: 0.25
- Rationale: The report uses a multicausal conclusion but does not perform the required comparative analysis or develop systemic collapse as a testable explanatory framework.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The summary says the collapse involved “multiple factors including climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, and political instability.”
  - The conclusion calls these “multiple interrelated contributing factors.”
  - The report explicitly notes that systemic collapse remains an “open research gap.”
- Missing:
  - No explicit and consistently applied comparison criteria are used.
  - Temporal fit, geographic reach, mechanism, evidentiary strength, survival, and adaptation are not compared across hypotheses.
  - The report does not classify factors as direct causes, contributors, consequences, or feedback mechanisms in a systematic way.
  - Systemic or cascading collapse is not presented as an integrative model; it is merely identified as insufficiently researched.
  - The conclusion favors all listed factors without demonstrating their relative explanatory power.

### R8

- Coverage: 0.25
- Depth: 0.25
- Rationale: The conclusion gestures toward uncertainty and multiple causation, but lacks the regional comparison and proportionate claim-by-claim assessment required.
- Candidate evidence:
  - The report describes the collapse as complex and says that some causes are “strongly supported” while others are “speculative or insufficiently explored.”
  - The conclusion uses uncertainty language, stating that systemic collapse is an “open research gap.”
- Missing:
  - No divergent regional cases of collapse, continuity, adaptation, or reorganization are compared.
  - The report does not use regional outcomes to test climate, warfare, migration, trade, or political explanations.
  - Its conclusion says there is “clearer support” for climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, and political instability without ranking or individually qualifying those claims.
  - It does not clearly separate well-supported findings, plausible but unresolved hypotheses, and speculation for each factor.
  - It does not address why some societies survived or reorganized while others experienced institutional failure.
