# Research Report

## Research Question

What caused the Late Bronze Age collapse? Compare the evidence for climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, political instability, and systemic collapse, and distinguish well-supported explanations from speculation.

## Summary

The Late Bronze Age collapse was a complex event influenced by multiple factors including climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, and political instability. While some causes are strongly supported by evidence, others remain speculative or insufficiently explored.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Climate change likely contributed to the Late Bronze Age collapse, causing droughts and agricultural failures that eroded the social and political stability of civilizations.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence reinforces the established connection between climate change and the societal collapse during the Late Bronze Age.

**Evidence**

- Research indicates that climate shifts and drought impacted agricultural systems, contributing to societal decline. [S3]
- Tree ring analysis shows severe droughts in the Hittite region coinciding with the collapse, likely causing famines. [S5]
- Severe droughts led to agricultural failures, which historians have argued contributed to the collapse. [S6]
- The study highlights the role of environmental factors including drought in precipitating societal decline, supporting the claim of climate change as a significant factor. [S7]
- The interdisciplinary study emphasizes climate shifts and environmental factors as significant elements contributing to the Late Bronze Age collapse. [S10]
- This research discusses the multifaceted causes of the Late Bronze Age collapse, emphasizing climate change, natural disasters, and water scarcity as significant factors. [S12]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

The invasion of the Sea Peoples contributed significantly to the Late Bronze Age collapse by disrupting trade networks and attacking cities.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While the new evidence provides additional context, it does not significantly change the understanding of the Sea Peoples' impact.

**Evidence**

- Historical accounts document invasions by the Sea Peoples during the period, leading to the destruction of major cities. [S2]
- Analysis of archaeological findings reveals attacks on urban centers that coincided with the timeline of the collapse. [S4]
- Invasions and attacks by the Sea Peoples are cited as contributing to urban destruction and trade disruptions. [S6]
- The research underscores the role of invasions and migration alongside trade disruptions during the period leading to the collapse, reinforcing the claim regarding the Sea Peoples. [S10]
- The paper examines the role of the Sea Peoples in disrupting trade networks and urban centers during the Late Bronze Age collapse. [S12]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Internal rebellions and political instability significantly contributed to the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

New evidence corroborates existing claims regarding political instability contributing to the collapse.

**Evidence**

- Research suggests that political failures in managing famine and social unrest were prevalent before the collapse. [S3]
- Accounts of inner strife and class wars were documented in conjunction with the timeline of the collapse. [S4]
- The article notes significant political turmoil and governance issues as contributing factors, aligning with indications of internal strife. [S12]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Crisis-induced migration played a role in the Late Bronze Age collapse, with evidence suggesting that forced movements were linked to warfare and political instability.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The correlation between crises and migration patterns is reinforced but requires further exploration.

**Evidence**

- The work discusses how forced migrations, arising from crises such as warfare, affected societies during the Late Bronze Age, indicating a connection to the collapse. [S8]
- The study indicates that migration patterns, driven by environmental and political crises, were integral to understanding the collapse dynamics. [S10]
- The research highlights how crises induced migration affected the dynamics of societal collapse. [S12]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Severe droughts during the Late Bronze Age led to widespread famine, significantly contributing to the social and economic instability that precipitated the collapse.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The research corroborates previous findings and strengthens the claim regarding drought-related famine.

**Evidence**

- Archaeological and palaeo-climatic evidence indicates severe droughts struck the Eastern Mediterranean, leading to failed harvests and widespread famine, corroborated by tree-ring analysis and pollen records. [S9]
- Discussion of environmental shifts impacting agricultural patterns is outlined, affirming the link between drought, famine, and societal collapse. [S10]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Trade disruptions during the Late Bronze Age contributed significantly to the collapse by destabilizing economies reliant on complex trade networks.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

This reinforces existing claims about trade disruptions but highlights a need for further evidence to clarify effects.

**Evidence**

- Collapse of trade routes crippled economies reliant on imported materials and caused social unrest, evidenced by communications from Ugarit appealing for aid amid impending disaster. [S9]
- Discussion on trade disruptions as a consequence of invasions and internal unrest is referenced, reinforcing the economic impact on societies. [S12]

### Finding 7

**Claim**

The Sea Peoples' invasions, coupled with internal revolts, further destabilized already weakened political entities, contributing to the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Provides additional support for the claims regarding Sea Peoples but does not change % impact assessment significantly.

**Evidence**

- Invasions by the Sea Peoples are highlighted as a primary factor in the urban destruction and political fragmentation, exacerbating the crisis faced by these civilizations. [S9]
- The article reiterates the role of the Sea Peoples' invasions alongside internal conflicts as a major destabilizing factor. [S12]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- No material conflict was identified in the retrieved evidence.

## Remaining Gaps

- The role of systemic collapse remains inadequately researched, lacking a claim that thoroughly addresses how interrelated factors contributed to the overall decline.

## Conclusion

The analysis of evidence suggests multiple interrelated contributing factors to the Late Bronze Age collapse, with clearer support for climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, and political instability. However, the role of systemic collapse is still an open research gap.
