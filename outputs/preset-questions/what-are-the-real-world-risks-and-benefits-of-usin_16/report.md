# Research Report

## Research Question

What are the real-world risks and benefits of using synthetic data to train or fine-tune large language models? Focus on data quality, bias, and evaluation.

## Summary

This report summarizes the findings related to the use of synthetic data in training large language models (LLMs) and addresses the associated risks such as bias and model collapse, while noting the benefits in terms of data scarcity and performance enhancement, particularly in low-resource settings.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Synthetic data effectively addresses challenges related to data scarcity, privacy, and class imbalance in training machine learning models.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

New evidence reinforces previous claims regarding the benefits of synthetic data in various contexts, ensuring high confidence.

**Evidence**

- Synthetic data mirrors real distributions, aiding in data scarcity, privacy, and class imbalance. [S1]
- Synthetic data provides a cost-effective alternative to real data, improving fairness and generalizability. [S2]
- Synthesized data can significantly enhance low-resource tasks and enable data augmentation. [S8]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Synthetic data can introduce or amplify biases if not generated with care.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence provides strong methodological insights and empirical results that align with and reinforce existing claims regarding the risks of bias in synthetic datasets, further elucidating mitigation strategies.

**Evidence**

- Poorly designed generators can reproduce/exaggerate existing biases in synthetic datasets. [S2]
- Synthetic data, if poorly constructed, can lead to unfair models and balance issues. [S5]
- Synthetic data generation poses risks that need to be managed, as biases and inaccuracies can be amplified in synthetic datasets. [S6]
- Quality, diversity, and complexity are essential factors influencing the risks associated with synthetic data, including biases. [S9]
- Uncontrolled passive inheritance can lead to model collapse, amplifying biases and reducing model accuracy. [S22]
- Training on synthetic data can amplify existing biases, highlighting the need for careful management. [S23]
- Synthetic data can lead to model collapse and reinforce errors and biases when not properly managed. [S24]
- In-training bias mitigation techniques have shown greater effectiveness in reducing biases in large language models compared to post-training methods. [S32]
- Bias manifests in various forms within LLMs, necessitating robust methods for assessment and mitigation. [S33]
- Synthetic data can address bias issues by augmenting training datasets, but it must be handled carefully to avoid introducing new biases into large ML models. [S34]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Model collapse is a significant risk when relying solely on synthetic data for training.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The new evidence reinforces existing concerns about model collapse, elaborating on its mechanisms and impacts related to synthetic data reliance.

**Evidence**

- Model collapse occurs when synthetic data replaces real data without sufficient real data retention. [S3]
- Evidence of model collapse raises concerns about reliance on synthetic data; results suggest synthetic data may negatively impact model performance over time. [S9]
- Model collapse is described as the degradation of machine learning models due to training on uncurated synthetic data, which leads to performance loss and diversity reduction. [S14]
- Model collapse occurs when AI models are trained on prior outputs of AI, creating a feedback loop that reduces output quality and diversity over generations. [S15]
- Recursive training using synthetic content can lead to model collapse, resulting in decreased performance and diversity in generated outputs. [S16]
- Model collapse is a progressive degradation of an AI model’s performance, heavily influenced by recursive training on AI-generated data. [S18]
- The article discusses the model collapse phenomenon and outlines how poorly designed synthetic data generation methods can severely affect model performance. [S19]
- Model collapse occurs as models trained on synthetic data lose quality and diversity, becoming less reliable. [S23]
- The feedback loop of training on synthetic data is a clear risk, emphasizing that it may lead to biased models if unaddressed. [S21]
- Fine-tuning models on synthetic data from diverse sources can mitigate distribution collapse, preserving the breadth of the output distribution and output text diversity. [S26]

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Synthetic data generated by large language models can enhance model performance, particularly in low-resource settings, by augmenting real-world datasets.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Additional evidence reinforces the applicability of synthetic data in enhancing model performance through improved diversity in training data compositions.

**Evidence**

- LLMs create task-relevant examples that augment or substitute for real datasets, especially where data is scarce, expensive, or sensitive. [S6]
- Pre-training with synthetic data mixed with natural web texts can significantly speed up convergence in training large language models. [S8]
- LLMs produce artificial, task-relevant examples, significantly augmenting datasets and enhancing performance in scenarios with scarce data. [S10]
- Research shows that synthetic data diversity significantly impacts LLM performance in both pre-training and fine-tuning stages, demonstrating benefits in low-resource settings. [S30]

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Challenges remain in ensuring the quality and diversity of synthetic data, particularly regarding biases and factual inaccuracies in generated outputs.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Recent evidence highlights the complexities of ensuring quality and diversity in synthetic datasets, thereby reinforcing prior claims.

**Evidence**

- Synthetic data generation poses risks of factual inaccuracies and biases; mitigation strategies are required for ethical and quality safeguards. [S6]
- Quality, diversity, and complexity are essential characteristics of synthetic data that impact model generalization, with significant trade-offs involved. [S9]
- Using low-quality synthetic data can lead to model collapse, which compromises the ability to generalize and maintain quality. [S24]

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Model collapse risks persist when synthetic data is overly relied upon, leading to loss in diversity and robustness during training processes.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The evidence further emphasizes the risks associated with excessive reliance on synthetic data and the consequent model collapse phenomenon.

**Evidence**

- Concerns persist around model collapse due to recursive training on synthetic data, suggesting degradation in quality and diversity. [S8]
- Recursive training on generated data progressively reduces diversity and performance, affirming risks associated with excessive reliance on synthetic data. [S11]
- Sufficient diversity in synthetic data sources can mitigate model collapse, preserving output quality and diversity of language models. [S12]
- Diverse synthetic data sources positively impact the robustness of language models during training, helping to prevent model collapse. [S13]
- Model collapse leads to a loss of diversity and performance, particularly when generative models are recursively trained on their own outputs. [S15]
- Recursive reliance on synthetic data risks eroding the quality and diversity of language models, accelerating the model collapse phenomenon. [S18]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- No material conflict was identified in the retrieved evidence.

## Remaining Gaps

- No major remaining gap was identified within the research scope.

## Conclusion

The findings underscore the dual-edged nature of synthetic data in training LLMs. While it provides significant advantages in addressing data scarcity and enhancing model performance in low-resource contexts, it also introduces risks related to bias and model collapse. Continuous efforts in quality management, diversity in synthetic data generation, and careful evaluation techniques are vital to mitigate these risks.
