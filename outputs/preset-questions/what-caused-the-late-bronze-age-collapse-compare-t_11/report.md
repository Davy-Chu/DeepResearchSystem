# Research Report

## Research Question

What caused the Late Bronze Age collapse? Compare the evidence for climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, political instability, and systemic collapse, and distinguish well-supported explanations from speculation.

## Summary

The Late Bronze Age (circa 1200-1150 BCE) collapse was a complex event characterized by the fall of several ancient civilizations such as the Hittites, Mycenaeans, and Egyptians. This report synthesizes the evidence supporting various explanations including climate change, warfare, migration, trade disruption, political instability, and systemic collapse, distinguishing between well-supported claims and those that remain speculative.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Climate change contributed to the Late Bronze Age collapse.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Paleo-climatic records and archaeological data provide a consistent pattern of drought impacting agricultural societies.

**Evidence**

- Paleo-climatic data indicate significant drought periods in the eastern Mediterranean during this time, which would have affected agricultural production.
- Archaeological findings suggest a decrease in population coinciding with periods of drought, reinforcing the link between climate and societal stress.

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Warfare played a significant role in the collapse.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Archaeological evidence of destruction layers and contemporaneous written records support the theory of significant military conflict.

**Evidence**

- Evidence of mass destruction at archaeological sites corresponds with accounts of invasions such as the Sea Peoples' incursions.
- Hittite and Mycenaean records mention ongoing conflicts and defense preparations in the years leading to the collapse, indicating a societal focus on warfare.

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Migration of peoples led to instability and contributed to societal collapse.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While migration is evident, the extent to which it triggered or was a response to the collapse remains debated.

**Evidence**

- Artifacts and population shifts during the Late Bronze Age align with movements of groups like the Sea Peoples, suggesting widespread migration.

### Finding 4

**Claim**

Trade disruption was a primary factor in the collapse.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Archaeological evidence shows a sharp decline in imported goods, indicating significant disruptions to trade routes.

**Evidence**

- The collapse of trade networks is evidenced by the decline in shared material culture and the economic decline in city-states dependent on trade.

### Finding 5

**Claim**

Political instability contributed to the collapse of Bronze Age civilizations.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While political changes are evident, the direct link between instability and collapse is complex and multifaceted.

**Evidence**

- Evidence of weakened central authority and localized power struggles suggests political fragmentation leading up to the collapse.

### Finding 6

**Claim**

Systemic collapse due to the interconnectedness of Bronze Age societies led to a cascade of failures.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The systemic nature of Bronze Age societies creates a compelling argument for interconnected failures leading to collapse.

**Evidence**

- The interdependence of trade and agriculture means that disruptions in one area would have significant repercussions in others, leading to a systemic crisis.

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- The role of the Sea Peoples is debated; while they are often blamed for destruction, their exact contribution to the collapse is unclear.
- Drought's impact on societal collapse can be difficult to quantify against other factors like warfare.
- The timing and impact of the various proposed causes (climate, trade, warfare) can overlap, making it hard to attribute the collapse to a single factor.

## Remaining Gaps

- Further archaeological exploration in the eastern Mediterranean to better understand the chronological and causal relationships between factors.
- Analysis of climate data at a finer resolution to correlate local conditions with societal responses.
- More interdisciplinary studies integrating historical, archaeological, and climatic data to construct a holistic view of the collapse.

## Conclusion

The Late Bronze Age collapse is attributed to a combination of climate change, warfare, trade disruption, and systemic issues, with well-supported evidence for each. While speculation exists regarding migration and political instability as direct causes, the interconnected and multifaceted nature of these factors presents a compelling narrative of systemic failure rather than a singular event.
