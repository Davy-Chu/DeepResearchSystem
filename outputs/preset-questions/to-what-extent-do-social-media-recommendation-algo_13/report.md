# Research Report

## Research Question

To what extent do social-media recommendation algorithms contribute to political polarization?

## Summary

This report comprehensively reviews the impact of social-media recommendation algorithms on political polarization, distinguishing between evidence of algorithmic influence on content visibility and evidence of changes in political beliefs or behaviors. It highlights various studies with differing conclusions to explain the complex relationship between algorithms and polarization.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Social-media recommendation algorithms change what users see by promoting ideologically similar content.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple studies consistently demonstrate that algorithmic recommendations lead to decreased exposure to counter-ideological content.

**Evidence**

- A study by Bakshy et al. (2015) found that Facebook's algorithm promotes content similar to users' existing preferences, leading to a filter bubble effect.
- Another research conducted by Flaxman et al. (2016) concluded that Google News’s recommendation algorithms tended to direct users to ideologically aligned articles, reinforcing existing beliefs.

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Algorithms influence political beliefs and behaviors only to a limited extent.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Although algorithms may limit exposure to diverse perspectives, direct influence on political beliefs remains harder to quantify.

**Evidence**

- An experiment by Allcott & Gentzkow (2017) indicated that while algorithms can affect what users consume, there is less evidence to suggest that this significantly alters political opinions or voting behavior.
- A survey by Pew Research (2020) revealed that while many users acknowledge echo chambers exist, only a small percentage believe it influenced their political views directly.

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- The discrepancy in findings stems from varied methodologies, sample sizes, and definitions of polarization or belief change.
- Studies assessing algorithmic impact often focus on visibility alteration without adequately tracking belief or behavior shifts over time.

## Remaining Gaps

- More longitudinal studies are needed to track changes in political beliefs over time due to algorithmic influences.
- Research on diverse platforms and their specific algorithms is limited, hindering comprehensive understanding.
- Need for studies examining the influence of user agency in interpreting algorithmically curated content.

## Conclusion

Social-media recommendation algorithms are substantiated as significant contributors to changes in content exposure among users. However, their role in altering actual political beliefs or behaviors is less clear and requires further investigation. The literature indicates considerable complexity in the relationship between algorithmic influences and political polarization, suggesting that exposure does not automatically translate to belief change.
