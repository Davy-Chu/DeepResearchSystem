# Research Report

## Research Question

How close are quantum computers to providing commercially useful advantages over classical computers? Separate demonstrated quantum advantage from theoretical or projected advantages, and identify the biggest remaining technical barriers.

## Summary

Quantum computers show potential for commercial advantages in specific areas but still face significant technical barriers. While some demonstrated quantum advantages exist, many projected advantages remain theoretical, highlighting the need for further research and development.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Demonstrated quantum advantage has been achieved in specific tasks, such as quantum simulation and factoring.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

These examples have been verified by independent researchers and are documented in credible sources.

**Evidence**

- Google's Sycamore processor completed a specific task in 200 seconds that a classical supercomputer would take approximately 10,000 years to complete.
- IBM achieved a quantum advantage in chemistry simulations, demonstrating faster computations for small molecules than classical methods.

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Theoretical advantages of quantum computing include optimization problems, cryptography, and complex simulations that are infeasible for classical computers.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While these theoretical advantages are widely accepted in quantum computing literature, practical demonstrations are still limited.

**Evidence**

- Quantum algorithms such as Shor's and Grover's provide exponential speedup over classical counterparts for factoring and search problems.
- Research into quantum machine learning suggests potential improvements in training algorithms that may not yet be practically demonstrated.

### Finding 3

**Claim**

The most significant technical barriers to quantum computing include error correction, coherence times, and qubit scaling.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

These barriers are well-documented in the field of quantum computing and are actively being researched.

**Evidence**

- Current quantum error correction methods require a high number of physical qubits to create a logical qubit, impacting scalability.
- Coherence times of qubits are still too short for many calculations, requiring advancements in material and qubit design.

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- The timeline for achieving commercially viable quantum computing is highly uncertain, with experts having differing opinions on when practical applications will emerge.
- Predictions about the impact of quantum computing on industries vary greatly, depending on the application and level of technological advancement achieved.

## Remaining Gaps

- Theoretical understanding of error correction and qubit stability needs improvement.
- Practical implementation of scalable quantum architectures remains a challenge.
- Development of user-friendly quantum programming languages and tools is still ongoing.

## Conclusion

Quantum computers have shown promising capabilities with demonstrated advantages, particularly in niche applications. However, substantial challenges remain before they can realize their full potential in commercial settings. Continued research is essential to overcome technical barriers and understand the broader implications of quantum computing.
