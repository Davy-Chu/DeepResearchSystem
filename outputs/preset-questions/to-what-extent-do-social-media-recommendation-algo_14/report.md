# Research Report

## Research Question

To what extent do social-media recommendation algorithms contribute to political polarization? Separate evidence that algorithms change what users see from evidence that they actually change political beliefs or behavior, and explain why the literature reaches different conclusions.

## Summary

Social media algorithms significantly influence what content users see, thereby contributing to political polarization. Various studies document how the curation of content amplifies emotionally charged and sensationalist posts, which may lead users to perceive their political views as more prevalent. Additionally, while algorithms can shift political attitudes, evidence indicates that exposure to like-minded content often has a greater impact than the algorithms themselves. The literature reflects mixed findings regarding the direct influence of algorithms, highlighting the complexity of interactions between algorithms and user behaviors.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Social media algorithms shape the political information users encounter, exacerbating political polarization.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Multiple sources consistently indicate that algorithms influence content visibility, fostering polarization.

**Evidence**

- Algorithms curate content based on user behavior, selectively amplifying sensationalist and emotionally charged posts. [S1] [S4] [S13]
- Users exposed to algorithmically curated environments view their political beliefs as more common, increasing polarization. [S1] [S2]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Algorithmic changes can influence political attitudes and beliefs.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

While algorithms do influence attitudes, conflicting findings about their overall impact necessitate caution.

**Evidence**

- Users on an algorithmic feed showed shifts toward more conservative views compared to those on a chronological feed. [S1] [S3]
- Exposure to algorithmically selected arguments increased polarization, but less so than exposure to like-minded content. [S2]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Exposure to like-minded content impacts political beliefs more significantly than algorithms alone.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

Conflicting evidence exists about the comparative strength of user interactions versus algorithmic influence on beliefs.

**Evidence**

- Interaction with like-minded individuals creates stronger shifts in beliefs compared to exposure to algorithmically curated content. [S2] [S3]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- Some studies suggest algorithms amplify polarization directly, while others indicate that exposure to like-minded content has a stronger effect. [S2] [S3]
- Contradictory findings suggest that while algorithms influence attitudes, user interactions may play a more significant role. [S2] [S3]

## Remaining Gaps

- What specific features of social media algorithms contribute to increases in political polarization?
- How do individual differences in user behavior interact with algorithmic curation?
- What specific aspects of algorithm design, such as engagement metrics or filtering criteria, have the most pronounced effects on political polarization?

## Conclusion

Social media algorithms play a critical role in shaping political polarization by influencing content visibility and user interactions. While algorithms do affect political attitudes, the significant role of user behavior and engagement with like-minded content complicates direct attributions of polarization solely to algorithmic influence. Ongoing research is essential to clarify these dynamics.
