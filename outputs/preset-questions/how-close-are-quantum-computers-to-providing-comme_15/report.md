# Research Report

## Research Question

How close are quantum computers to providing commercially useful advantages over classical computers? Separate demonstrated quantum advantage from theoretical or projected advantages, and identify the biggest remaining technical barriers.

## Summary

This report addresses the current state of quantum computing in relation to its commercial viability. It distinguishes between demonstrated practical advantages and theoretical potential while highlighting ongoing technical challenges.

## Findings

### Finding 1

**Claim**

Quantum computers have recently achieved practical quantum advantage in materials simulation, delivering performance benefits over classical machines.

**Confidence:** Medium

**Why this confidence level**

The claim is supported by new evidence from a recent demonstration but is based on specific applications.

**Evidence**

- Q-CTRL has demonstrated practical quantum advantage by achieving a 3,000 times speedup in materials simulation, making this relevant to commercial applications. [S2]
- Quantum redundant calculations have shown that quantum systems can outperform classical methods in specific use cases. [S4]

### Finding 2

**Claim**

Quantum advantage, while theoretically understood, is still elusive in many practical applications, with significant regional and task-specific barriers present.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

The claim is well-supported by multiple independent evaluations and established concerns in the field.

**Evidence**

- Research indicates that the current landscape of quantum computing shows clear results primarily for large or complex problems. [S5]
- Benchmarks highlight that while quantum algorithms are promising, classical methods remain superior for small to moderate problems. [S4]

### Finding 3

**Claim**

Major technical barriers for quantum computing include hardware limitations, error rates, and the need for specialized environments to maintain qubit stability.

**Confidence:** High

**Why this confidence level**

Numerous sources agree on the significance of these technical issues as persistent and critical barriers.

**Evidence**

- Superconducting and trapped-ion technologies require extreme conditions to function, leading to inherent challenges in scaling quantum computers for practical use. [S3]
- Noise and error correction are critical issues that quantum systems currently face, affecting reliable performance in computations. [S2]

## Conflicts and Uncertainty

- There are differing opinions on when practical quantum advantage will become more widespread. Some sources claim immediate viability while others project that significant breakthroughs are still years away. [S2] [S5]

## Remaining Gaps

- What specific industries will benefit most from the practical advantages of quantum computing?
- How long until quantum systems can routinely outperform classical systems across a broader range of applications?

## Conclusion

Quantum computing has made notable strides towards practical advantages over classical computing in specific applications like materials simulation, but significant barriers remain. The industry is progressing toward greater commercial viability, yet timelines for widespread application still vary widely among experts.
